## Local facts established by reading the tree at `eed2014`

1. **Dictionary collocations are already symmetric units in the vocabulary ranking.** `PooledWords` reads the reference corpus through `LegibilityReading`, which applies `CollocatedWords`, so `reference-corpus-shares.tsv` carries ~886 collocated rows (`access_token` 277, `test_case` 599, `credit_card` 105 …) and this repository's own ranking already lists 19 collocated units, `part_of_speech` at 15 occurrences standing. The commission's sentence "every Jensen–Shannon figure rests on single-word atoms" is exact for the placement and scope divergences, and one step out of date for the vocabulary ranking, whose unit is already "word or published dictionary run" on both sides.
2. **What feeds no distribution is the taxonomy-term path.** `PublishedPhrases` pools only WordNet multiword lemmas and Wiktionary collocations (69,713). The term vocabularies with the 10–100× field-to-control separation — FIBO, FpML, FIX, CSO, OLiA, CWE — are matched by `TermSpans` at three normalisation normalisations (WORDS, LEMMAS, SENSES) and their matches reach `evidence.json` `matches` and nothing else.
3. **The `matches` record already carries what direction 2 needs**: vocabulary, term, wordsInTerm, normalisation, specificity, occurrences, outcome (`REPORTED` / `REFUSED_BY_BRANCH_RULE`), concepts, sites. This reading: 510 matches, 230 reported, 280 refused by the branch rule.
4. **The uncounted 0.5 of direction 2 is `DomainMasses.UNCOUNTED_SENSE`**, on the venn pages. Every sense a subject scheme supplies is uncounted (`SubjectDomains` and `TopicLabelDomains` build `new CountedSenseDomains(domains, 0)`), so all CSO/arXiv/OpenAlex senses weigh 0.5 while WordNet senses weigh their SemCor counts. The engine's own reading still takes the commonest sense (`SenseDomains.fromClasspath`), so the counted-weight arithmetic exists only page-side.
5. **The placing label is dropped exactly where direction 1 says.** `SubjectDomains`/`TopicLabelDomains` label each sense with the subject's *ancestor* concepts of a stated level and never keep the subject's own label — the phrase that placed the word. `CountedSenseDomains` is `(domains, uses, labelStrength)` and has no field for it.
6. **A phrase-aware reference is derivable locally.** The 100 corpus clones are on disk at `~/corpus` (128 directories, 3.0 GB), and `CorpusPoolCommand` regenerates the table through the same reading path. Extending `PublishedPhrases` therefore changes both sides of the vocabulary divergence in one move — the symmetric place `never-filter-the-corpus-alone` requires — at compute cost only, no bandwidth.
7. **`PhraseTopics` already pools one unit per phrase** (declared name or sentence), geometric mean over agreeing words times the agreeing share, file prior as a `[1, 2]` factor, credence from sense coverage. `TopicCommitment` gives each word Σp² total voice. A published run inside a name is one "word" to all of this once `CollocatedWords` has merged it — but only runs the two topical dictionaries label; taxonomy terms never merge.
8. **The scale of the current merge on this repository**: 127 collocated units in `workings.words` (`part_of_speech` 42, `computer_science` 36, `set_aside` 22 …).

## Literature review 1 of 5: one sense per collocation, one sense per discourse, multiword anchors (all figures verified against fetched primary PDFs unless marked)

- **Yarowsky 1993, [One Sense Per Collocation](https://aclanthology.org/H93-1052.pdf)** (binary ambiguities, 380M-word corpora, five sense definitions, mean sample 3,944, mean prior .69): an ambiguous word has one sense in a given collocation with probability **90–99%**; mean precision across collocation types **95%**; combined disambiguator **92% precision at 98% coverage**. Per collocation type (precision/recall): content word immediately right **.97/.29** (noun .98), immediately left **.96/.26** (**noun .99**, the strongest cell); adjective↔noun .98/.54; first content word right .94/.51. Cross-validated entropy of Pr(Sense|Collocation): adjacent content word .18–.24 bits. **A single 1/0 observation on the adjacent-word collocate already classifies at >92%**; 15/0 approaches perfect; the same distribution 30 words away is worth 3% above chance. Information decays steeply with distance for verbs/adjectives; persists for nouns.
- **Gale, Church & Yarowsky 1992, [One Sense Per Discourse](https://aclanthology.org/H92-1045.pdf)**: same-discourse same-sense **98%** (raw blind-judged 51/54 ≈ 94%, adjusted by an assumed 60/40 unambiguous mixture); Brown replication 102/106 ≈ 96%. Yarowsky 1995 re-measured the constraint at **99.8% accuracy, 50.1% applicability** over 37,232 hand-tagged examples.
- **Yarowsky 1995, [Unsupervised WSD Rivaling Supervised Methods](https://aclanthology.org/P95-1026.pdf)**: seed collocations (dictionary-definition words, or one defining collocate per sense, WordNet named as a source) → decision list ranked by log-likelihood ratio, classification by the **single most predictive matching evidence, never combined** — deliberately robust to dependent evidence. One-sense-per-discourse applied **probabilistically with counted evidence**, not as a hard rewrite: at n ≥ 4 occurrences in a discourse all but the most confident local tags are overridden, at n = 2 little reclassification. Final: 96.5% unsupervised vs 96.1% supervised twin; discourse constraint = 27% error reduction.
- **Finlayson & Kulkarni 2011, [Detecting MWEs improves WSD](https://aclanthology.org/W11-0805.pdf)**: WordNet 1.6 multiword lemmas average polysemy **1.07 versus 1.53** for single words; *world* 9 senses, *record* 14, *world record* **1**. The standing convention (describing Arranz, Atserias & Castillo 2005): a detected WordNet MWE **replaces its constituent tokens as the disambiguation unit**, because missing it "spuriously assign[s] senses to MWE constituents". Longest-match left-to-right is "the most common strategy for MWE-aware WSD". Effect on most-frequent-sense WSD over SemCor: **+3.3 F1** with baseline longest-match detection, +5.0 best, +6.1 with perfect detection (0.579 → 0.640) — nearly all attainable gain comes from simple detection.
- **Vasilescu & Langlais, [TALN 2004](https://aclanthology.org/2004.jeptalnrecital-long.20/)** (Senseval-2 all-words, 2,473 words): simplified Lesk — context against the sense's **own published description** — P **58.18** at ±2, degrading as the window widens; original Lesk 42.6–43.3; most-frequent-sense baseline 57.99. The winning variant is a "silent strategy": it decides only on genuine overlap. Overlap with a sense's own label text is high-precision, low-coverage evidence — a vote strong when present and absent otherwise.
- **Magnini et al. 2001 ([S01-1027](https://aclanthology.org/S01-1027.pdf))**: P .748 / R .357 confirmed; the paper says **nothing about multiwords** — must not be cited for phrase anchoring. Its lesson: domain evidence alone answers ~36% of words.
- **Babelfy ([Q14-1019](https://aclanthology.org/Q14-1019.pdf))**: the alternative to hard collapse — keep overlapping fragments (*Major League Soccer*, *major league*, *league*, *soccer*) as competing candidates and let a coherence score decide; superstring matching for entities 98.6% vs 42.4% exact-match on KORE50.
- UNVERIFIED from this review: the "~88% of WordNet words monosemous" overall figure; Arranz et al. 2005 read only through Finlayson & Kulkarni; Lesk 1986's original text; UKB's MWE handling.

## Literature review 2 of 5: multiword-expression recognition and evaluation

All quotes verified against fetched primary PDFs unless marked.

- **[Constant et al. 2017, MWE Processing: A Survey](https://aclanthology.org/J17-4005.pdf)** (CL 43(4)): **discovery** = "finding *new* MWEs (types) in text corpora … a lexicon"; **identification** = "automatically annotating MWEs (tokens) in running text by associating them with known MWEs (types)". Lexicon-based identification by "maximum forward matching … the longest matching MWE … tends to overgenerate" — via lemma-level normalisation admitting forbidden variants (*prendre la porte* singular-only), and via **literal uses** of listed expressions ("*I realize now that he never looks up…*"). Longest-match resolves nesting; partial overlap (*pay₁ close₂ attention₁,₂*) is "usually ignored" as sufficiently rare. Discontiguity and coordination-overlap are the other error modes. Supervised identification "do[es] not perform well on rare MWEs". No numeric P/R for bare lookup in the survey.
- **[Sag et al. 2002](https://verbs.colorado.edu/~mpalmer/Ling7800/SagMWE.pdf)** (CICLing): "In WordNet 1.7 … **41% of the entries are multiword** … almost certainly an underestimate." Jackendoff 1997:156: MWEs in a speaker's lexicon are of the same order of magnitude as single words. "Specialized domain vocabulary, such as terminology, **overwhelmingly consists of MWEs**… the proportion of MWEs will rise as the system adds vocabulary for new domains." Classification: lexicalized (fixed / semi-fixed / syntactically-flexible) vs institutionalised phrases. For **fixed expressions** "a simple words-with-spaces representation is sufficient" — and an identifier cannot inflect, modify internally, or take gaps, so every identifier match behaves as a fixed expression; the flexibility/proliferation objections apply to running text.
- **Baldwin & Kim 2010** (Handbook of NLP ch.): WHOLLY UNVERIFIED — no fetchable copy; do not quote.
- **[PARSEME edition 1.2 — Ramisch et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.mwe-1.14.pdf)**: metrics = MWE-based (exact-span) and token-based P/R/F1; **unseen** = multiset of lemmas annotated in test but absent from train+dev (~300 per language). Best system MTLB-STRUCT: global MWE-based F1 **70.1**, unseen F1 **38.5**. **Seen2Seen** — non-neural, rule-based, matching only expressions already in its lexicon — posts the **highest global MWE-based precision of any system in either track, 76.2** (R 58.6, F1 66.2), with unseen recall 0.6: a lexicon matcher contributes essentially nothing outside its lexicon. Per-language best F1 correlates with the unseen ratio at **Pearson −0.90** — lexicon coverage, not matcher cleverness, moves identification. (Ramisch et al. 2018 formula details UNVERIFIED.)
- **MWE identification in source-code identifiers: no published work found** — one search round; absence-of-evidence, not a verified negative.
- **What transfers to identifiers**: the task is identification, not discovery; of the survey's four error modes only literal use and nesting/overlap can arise in identifiers (no inflection, no gaps, no coordination); the documented profile of pure lexicon matching — best-in-field precision on stated entries, silence elsewhere — is the doctrine's own shape ("a reading that cannot cite abstains").

## Literature review 3 of 5: collocation measures, bounds, reference-free nulls

All formulas verified against fetched primary PDFs unless marked.

- **PMI — [Church & Hanks 1990](https://aclanthology.org/J90-1003.pdf)**: I(x,y) = log2[P(x,y)/(P(x)P(y))], joint estimated from a 5-word window over the corpus itself. The paper's own instability rule: "we will not discuss word pairs with f(x,y) ≤ 5 … the simple but arbitrary threshold". No derived bound — the maximum is the moving value −log2 P(x,y), growing as the pair gets rarer. Refusable here on the KL ground.
- **NPMI — [Bouma 2009](https://svn.spraakdata.gu.se/repos/gerlof/pub/www/Docs/npmi-pfd.pdf)**: NPMI = ln[p(x,y)/(p(x)p(y))] / (−ln p(x,y)). Bound [−1, 1] derived: p(x), p(y) ≥ p(x,y) gives PMI ≤ −ln p(x,y) (so ≤ 1, equality at perfect co-occurrence); p(x)p(y) ≤ 1 gives PMI ≥ ln p(x,y) (so ≥ −1, the never-together limit); independence gives 0. "PMI's alleged sensitivity to low frequency is perhaps more accurately described as a lack of sensitivity to high frequency." Empirically NPMI edges PMI on MWE-2008 gold sets (e.g. 44.6% → 45.4% average precision), demoting low-frequency pairs.
- **Dice / logDice — [Rychlý 2008](https://www.fi.muni.cz/usr/sojka/download/raslan2008/13.pdf)**: D = 2·fxy/(fx+fy); logDice = 14 + log2 D. Upper bound 14 derived from 2·fxy ≤ fx+fy; "usually the value is less then 10"; +1 point = twice the collocation rate. The 14 offset is calibration anchoring 0 at 1 co-occurrence per 16,384. **No corpus size N anywhere in the formula** — needs only fxy, fx, fy. No lower bound (fxy = 0 undefined).
- **Log-likelihood G² — [Dunning 1993](https://aclanthology.org/J93-1003.pdf)**: −2 log λ, χ²₁ under the null, valid at low counts where t/z/χ² fail; the bigram table is four cells from the one corpus under study. Unbounded — a test statistic, not an effect size; G² = 2N·MI (Bouma fn. 4), i.e. 2N times a KL divergence, refused for reporting on exactly the KL ground.
- **Evert — [Corpora and Collocations, extended manuscript 2008](https://www.stephanie-evert.de/PUB/Evert2007HSK_extended_manuscript.pdf)**: contingency table O11, E11 = R1·C1/N all from one corpus. "MI … inflated scores to low-frequency word pairs with E ≪ 1 … even a single cooccurrence … might result in a fairly high association score"; effect-size measures "unreliable especially for low-frequency data"; frequency thresholds make them "much more useful". Fisher's exact test is "the most appropriate significance test for contingency tables" (after Pedersen 1996). And the caution that binds everything: "the null hypothesis of independence is extremely unrealistic. Words are never combined at random in natural language" ([Evert 2006, the library metaphor](https://www.stephanie-evert.de/PUB/Evert2008.pdf)) — so a permutation p ranks surprise, it does not certify collocation-hood; report a bounded effect statistic qualified by the null.
- **Pecina 2010 ([LREV 44](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10579-009-9101-4)), PARTIALLY VERIFIED (paywalled)**: 82 association measures compared; no single measure dominates; supervised combinations beat every individual measure. Bouma's verified text states the same lesson: "there is not one association measure that is best in all situations."
- **Reference-free nulls for phrase adjacency** (the "no phrase-frequency reference" answer): (1) shuffled-text surrogate — permute the corpus's own tokens, recompute adjacency statistics; the corpus's own unigram margins are the reference ([Montemurro & Zanette 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0019875), procedure-level quotes UNVERIFIED); (2) document-level permutation — the project's existing null in print ([arXiv:2308.13383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13383)), extends to phrase counts unchanged; (3) Fisher's exact conditioning on the observed margins, endorsed in Evert's verified chapter. All computable from the repository's own counts.
- **Leipzig Corpora Collection: UNVERIFIED** — download page behind a bot-challenge; licence, and whether neighbour-co-occurrence tables still ship (historically word-pair, count, log-likelihood columns), must be read in a normal browser. Data description: Goldhahn, Eckart & Quasthoff, LREC 2012. If bundled, the counts would be the admissible part, never the LL scores.

**The summary table (verified derivations):** measures needing only the repository's own counts: PMI (unbounded — refused), NPMI ([−1,1] — admissible), Dice ([0,1] — admissible), logDice (≤14 — admissible), minimum sensitivity ([0,1]), Fisher's exact p ([0,1]), permutation p ([0,1], floor 1/(n+1)); unbounded test statistics t/z/χ²/G² refused. An external reference corpus is needed only for keyness ("is this phrase common here relative to working Java") — which is where a re-pooled phrase-aware reference corpus, or Leipzig-style data, would enter.

## Literature review 4 of 5: termhood, unithood, term-bank lookup

- **Kageura & Umino 1996** (*Terminology* 3(2)): **termhood** = "the degree that a linguistic unit is related to (or, more straightforwardly, represents) domain-specific concepts"; **unithood** = "the degree of strength or stability of syntagmatic combinations and collocations". Unithood applies only to multi-word units; termhood to both. PARTIALLY VERIFIED — primary paywalled (403); framing confirmed in the fetched [Wong et al., IJCNLP 2008](https://aclanthology.org/I08-2084.pdf).
- **C-value ([Frantzi, Ananiadou & Mima 2000](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/sophia.ananiadou/IJODL2000.pdf) — primary is a scan; formula VERIFIED verbatim from two fetched restatements, [Marciniak & Mykowiecka, CompuTerm 2014](https://aclanthology.org/W14-4805.pdf) and [ATR4S, arXiv:1611.07804](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.07804))**: C-value(p) = log₂(length(p)) · freq(p) when p is nested in nothing; log₂(length(p)) · (freq(p) − (1/|Tₚ|)·Σ_{b∈Tₚ} freq(b)) when nested, Tₚ the set of longer matched phrases containing p. **Needs only the matches themselves** — frequencies, lengths, containment; the hand stop list belongs to the candidate-extraction front end, which a published term list replaces. Boundary behaviours: a term matched only inside one longer match scores 0 ("properly assumed to be incomplete"); a nested phrase in many distinct longer phrases scores higher at equal frequency. The log₂ length factor's stated rationale: "long phrases tend to occur more rarely than shorter ones" — citable precedent but a chosen form, patched twice in the literature (log₂(|t|+0.1); constant 1 for single words). NC-value's 0.8/0.2 split is a chosen constant; UNVERIFIED in the scan.
- **Weirdness ([Ahmad, Gillam & Tostevin, TREC-8](https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec8/papers/surrey2.pdf), fully VERIFIED)**: (ws/ts)/(wg/tg) against the BNC. Unbounded by its own table (weirdness 236,000 for *supercritical*); OOV handling undocumented in the primary; the operational thresholds (frequency > 2, weirdness > 100) hand-chosen with no derivation.
- **[ATR4S / Astrakhantsev 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.07804)** (LRE 52(3)): 13 methods, 7 datasets, average precision at K; "no single method demonstrates best average precision for all datasets"; PU-ATR most stable (avg AvP 0.5701), plain Weirdness 0.4566 out-averaging most sophisticated single measures. The paper's own conclusion: combine multiple features — weighted votes, this project's existing architecture.
- **Term variants — [Klavans & Jacquemin, DELOS](https://www.ercim.eu/publication/ws-proceedings/DELOS3/Jacquemin.pdf) (VERIFIED verbatim)**: rule-based variant matching against a controlled vocabulary: syntactic variants **91.2% precision**, morphosyntactic **86.4%**, combined **89.4%** at 75.2% recall; morphological variants expand indexing coverage ≥ **30%**. The motivating baseline: "only **65%** of the cooccurrences of content words within a 10-word window are genuine term variants" — a co-occurrence window with no grammar is one-third noise. (FASTR's 71,623-term list and +16.7% recall from insertion/permutation/coordination variants: UNVERIFIED.)
- **Dictionary lookup in biomedicine — Funk et al. 2014, [BMC Bioinformatics 15:59](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2105-15-59): ALL UNVERIFIED this session (fetch failed)** — reportedly ConceptMapper (pure dictionary lookup) best on most CRAFT ontologies, F ~0.8 where labels are long and distinctive, low where labels are short or common English. Verify before citing.
- **Usable, source-traceable, for weighing a match against an already-published term list**: (1) the nested-frequency correction needs nothing but the matches; (2) log₂ length is citable precedent, not a derived bound; (3) weirdness conflicts with the derived-bound doctrine explicitly; (4) no single score dominates — combine votes; (5) structured span/grammar matching against a controlled list is high-precision where bag-of-words proximity is 35% noise; (6) count distinct containing matches, not just contained frequency.

## Literature review 5 of 5: phrase-aware topic models and backoff smoothing

All mechanisms verified against fetched primary text unless marked.

- **[Wallach 2006](https://mimno.infosci.cornell.edu/info6150/readings/wallach06topic.pdf)** (ICML): bigram topic model — every word drawn conditioned on topic and previous word, no unigram fallback; better predictive accuracy than either parent model on two 150-document corpora; topics "less dominated by function words". Only shown effective on hundreds of documents.
- **[TNG — Wang, McCallum & Wei 2007](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~mccallum/papers/tng-icdm07.pdf)** (ICDM): per-token Bernoulli switch (conditioned on previous topic and word) between unigram topic distribution and topic-specific bigram distribution. Words in a phrase are NOT forced to one topic — the phrase's topic is read off the **last word (head noun)**; the enforced-consistency variant performed worse in-sampler. TREC ad-hoc: TNG alone AP 0.0709 vs LDA 0.0438 (+61.96%), significant (Wilcoxon 95%); mixed with a Dirichlet-smoothed query-likelihood LM.
- **[ToPMine — El-Kishky et al., PVLDB 8](http://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p305-ElKishky.pdf)**: phrase mining and document segmentation FIRST, then PhraseLDA over the segmented units. Merging significance (their Eq. 1): α(P₁,P₂) ≈ (f(P₁⊕P₂) − μ₀)/√f(P₁⊕P₂) with **μ₀ = L·p(P₁)·p(P₂) — the corpus's own independence expectation; no external phrase reference anywhere**. Agglomerative merge to a threshold; the result partitions each document into non-overlapping phrases and words. "By restricting all constituent terms within a phrase to share the same latent topic, we can assign a phrase the topic of its constituent words." Unigram-only topic lists called "hard-to-interpret or ambiguous"; expert study rates ToPMine highest on coherence. Runtime: ToPMine 67 s where PDLDA takes 3.72 h (sampled DBLP titles).
- **[PDLDA — Lindsey, Headden & Stipicevic 2012](https://aclanthology.org/D12-1020/)** (EMNLP): Boolean phrase-start variable partitions the corpus into consecutive non-overlapping n-grams; each phrase draws ONE topic; hierarchical Pitman-Yor word distributions. Verbatim criticism of TNG: "the aforementioned principle of non-compositionality supports the intuitive idea that each collocation ought to be drawn from a single topic." Evaluated by phrase intrusion, deliberately not perplexity. **Caution: one WebFetch of this paper returned a fabricated "15% perplexity improvement" figure, refuted by the extracted text — the fetch-summariser invented it.**
- **[SegPhrase — Liu et al., SIGMOD 2015](http://hanj.cs.illinois.edu/pdf/sigmod15_jliu.pdf)**: four quality signals verbatim — popularity, concordance ("collocation … significantly higher than what is expected due to chance"), informativeness ("'This paper' is popular and concordant … but not informative"), completeness ('vector machine' incomplete). **Rectified frequency**: segmentation partitions the text so each token occurrence counts inside exactly one phrase instance — raw frequency called "a critical bottleneck for all frequency-based quality assessment". 300 labelled phrases per corpus suffice to train its classifier.
- **[AutoPhrase — Shang et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04457)** (TKDE): distant supervision from a public knowledge base — Wikipedia titles, links and synonyms, "over 100,000 quality phrases" — replaces human labelling; robust positive-only training; POS-guided segmentation. **The direct precedent for "a published resource states which runs are phrases."** (The ~20-point recall advantage over SegPhrase on Spanish is read off PR curves — PARTIALLY VERIFIED.)
- **Backoff smoothing (verified via [Goodman, arXiv cs/0108005](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0108005); Katz 1987 original and Chen & Goodman TR-10-98 equations UNVERIFIED at source)**: **Katz**: use the discounted higher-order estimate where count > 0; otherwise back off, with α(history) handing exactly the Good-Turing-discounted mass to the lower order so the total still sums to 1 — the arithmetic answer to "how does the word remain the fallback without anything voting twice". **Jelinek-Mercer**: interpolation, every order votes always, weights sum to 1. **Kneser-Ney**: fallback weight is the continuation count — how many distinct contexts a word enters — because the fallback is only consulted where the higher order failed ("Francisco" is frequent but follows only "San"). "A modified interpolated form of Kneser-Ney smoothing consistently outperformed all other smoothing techniques" (Goodman, restating Chen & Goodman).
- **The precedents distilled**: (1) partition, then count — TNG, PDLDA, ToPMine and SegPhrase all make a token occurrence count inside exactly one unit; none lets a phrase and its words vote simultaneously; (2) one topic per phrase is the settled output convention (even TNG reports the phrase under its head noun's topic); (3) the word is the fallback and fallback mass is discounted, never duplicated (Katz/KN); (4) phrasehood can be decided by a published resource (AutoPhrase) or by the corpus's own independence expectation (ToPMine) — no phrase-frequency reference corpus appears in any of these systems.
