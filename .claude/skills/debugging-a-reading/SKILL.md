# Debugging a reading

A figure is explained when you can name the word that produced it, the resource that labelled that word, and the file and line it was written on. Anything short of that is a guess.

## Where the answer already is

Three files under `output/json/`, committed, regenerated by `./gradlew read`.

| File | Holds | Validated by |
|---|---|---|
| `reading.json` | the answers — placements with `carriedBy`, each carrying topic's two shares and up to three witnesses with quotations and file/line | `reading-export.schema.json`, before the file exists |
| `evidence.json` | the workings — `filesRead`, `scopes`, `edges`, the term `matches` with rung, specificity and outcome, and `workings`: every word offered with what each resource states about it, every topic a rule removed with the rule that removed it, and every run nothing read | nothing |
| `changes.json` | what moved since the last reading of the **same** `schemaVersion` | nothing |

**Check the schema version first.** `changes.json` is not written when the previous reading states a different `schemaVersion`, so a bumped schema means no comparison exists and its absence is not a finding.

**Start in `evidence.json`'s `workings`, not in a probe.** Four keys, and between them they answer most of what a probe used to be needed for.

| Key | Holds | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| `rules` | every rule that can remove a topic, with how many topics it removed and how many phrase readings | whether a rule is taking anything out at all, before you look for what |
| `words` | one row per word the reading offered, **ordered by what it carried**: `occurrences`, `carried`, `writtenAsAName`, first site, sense coverage, specificity, and what the resources state about it read three ways — `asHeadWord`, `inASentence`, `asVerb` — with the shares those came to | what a word was read as and what it was worth. A row whose three lists are all empty is a word no resource covers, stated rather than omitted |
| `removed` | one row per rule and topic, with occurrences and a handful of the phrases it happened in | a topic missing from an answer that the words plainly named |
| `unread` | every run nothing placed, kept as a run, with `NO_WORD_REACHED_A_RESOURCE` or `NO_RESOURCE_STATED_A_TOPIC` | which of this repository's own names carry no subject |

**Read `carried`, never `occurrences`.** They are two columns because they disagree by two orders of magnitude: across the nine evaluation-set members, 4,068,610 occurrences carry 169,335 of mass, so an average occurrence is worth 0.04. An occurrence's worth is settled *after* it is counted — `CopiedComments` prices prose standing in a hundred files at a hundredth of a sighting each, so a licence header is the commonest text in most repositories and nearly the quietest. Sorting by `occurrences` produces a list of Apache Licence 2.0 — `require`, `specific`, `condition`, `except`, `notice`, `fitness` — and none of it reaches any reading. Sorting by `carried` produces the repository's actual subject: `loan`, `charge`, `trade`, `gas`, `metadata`, `c14n`, `pom`.

This has cost a full round of wrong conclusions. `property` at 19,998 occurrences and `get` at 10,184 were reported as artefacts drowning a finance library's signal; neither is in that member's top six carriers, and `get` carries nothing at all.

**A word is read three ways and the three disagree.** `asHeadWord` is the word heading a declared name, `inASentence` is the same word in prose, `asVerb` is the leading word of a method name. Which of the three applied to an occurrence follows from where it was written, so all three are stated and the file chooses none. `read` as a verb carries `publishing`; `read` as a noun does not.

**`writtenAsAName` separates the two halves of a corpus.** A word the repository declared something with is vocabulary its author chose; a word only in prose is a sentence about something. The parse is what tells them apart and the column carries the answer.

## The probes

One Gradle invocation at a time. A background probe counts as one.

| Probe | Answers |
|---|---|
| `./gradlew wordVotes -Pwords="signature cluster"` | every topical vote the resources cast for a word, each vote's mass and source, the sense coverage, and the share each topic ends up with |
| `./gradlew topicCarriers <topic>` | every word that carried a topic, with occurrences and mass — the whole tail, not the witnesses the report shows |
| `./gradlew subjectWitness -Dcs.clone.dir=<path>` | what a placement rests on: the topics the repository and the subject both put mass in |
| `./gradlew publishedStatements -Parea="Computer Science" -Dcs.clone.dir=<path>` | a repository placed against each statement a scheme publishes, with the ranked topics, the band, and the field and subfield each rolls up to |
| `./gradlew armPlacement -Dcs.clone.dir=<path>` | every arm placed against every scheme, with the agreement between arms |
| `./gradlew readTimings` | stage timings — see [`profiling-a-read`](../profiling-a-read/SKILL.md) |

## The method

**Start at the bottom.** A placement is a distribution over some four hundred labels, so anything concluded from a placement, a band or a divergence is a guess about which words moved it. Read the words first and the figure second, every time.

Two traps sit in the words themselves, and both have been walked into:

- **`shareByTopic` is what the resources state about a word, not what the word contributed.** A word can be offered, recorded in `words`, and then discarded — the JavaBeans accessor prefix and the source-set name both return no votes, and neither removal is written down. `get` appears with `tennis 1.00` beside it in a repository where `tennis` is in no reading at all.
- **A frequency table does not demote a common word inside a name.** `OfferedWords.narrowing` returns `1.0` for any word of a declared name and consults `WordSpecificity` only for prose, because the author chose the name. So a common word in a name carries full weight whatever its rank says.

Then work down, never sideways. A placement rests on labels; a label rests on words; a word rests on a resource's statement about one of its senses.

1. **Take the placement's `carriedBy`** from `reading.json`. It names the labels and the two shares they met on.
2. **Ask what carried the label** — `topicCarriers`, or the witnesses already in the placement.
3. **Ask what the resources say about that word** — `wordVotes`. This is where most surprises resolve.
4. **Read the resource** if the vote is wrong. The bundled TSVs are greppable and every one states its provenance in its header.

**Where a topic is missing rather than wrong, work up instead.** Look for the topic in `workings.removed` before looking for the word that should have carried it: a topic a rule took out leaves no witness anywhere else.

### A worked example

Apache Santuario places at *Aerospace Engineering and Applications*, carried by `computing, medicine, linguistics, software`. `linguistics` in an XML security library is the surprise.

`./gradlew wordVotes -Pwords="signature cryptography encryption"` gives it:

| Word | Votes |
|---|---|
| `signature` | `linguistics` 0.42, `law` 0.42, `music` 0.04, `cryptography` 0.04, `medicine` 0.04, `printing` 0.04 |
| `cryptography` | **none — 0 votes, coverage 0.000** |
| `encryption` | `cryptography` 1.00 |

So the library's central concept votes 84% for linguistics and law, and the word naming its whole field votes nothing. The placement is a faithful reading of what the dictionaries say; the defect is upstream of the placement. Grepping the topic vocabulary then shows `cryptography` is a label it publishes and not a headword it labels — and that 470 of its 719 labels are the same.

## Traps, each one paid for

**A green build in nine seconds is cached.** Run `./gradlew cleanTest checkAll` and gate on the `BUILD SUCCESSFUL` line, not an exit code. Then check the tests actually ran: `grep '^> Task .*:test' `, and count the results — a module reported `FROM-CACHE` ran nothing.

**A silent no-op looks exactly like a change.** `python3` `str.replace` returns the string unchanged when the pattern does not match, and the file still compiles. After any scripted edit, grep for the new text before believing it landed. An edit that assumed `subject.group()` where the code said `subject.module()` cost a full measurement round and produced figures that were byte-identical to the baseline.

**Byte-identical figures mean the change is not biting.** Two variants that produce the same number to four decimal places have not been compared. Find the reason before interpreting the result — usually the probe constructs its own input and bypasses the path that changed.

**A verdict inside the null's jitter is a coin toss.** Any placement against a large field draws its bar from a resample. Re-run at three or four seeds — the probes take a seed as a second argument — before reporting *stands apart*. At 4,516 subjects the bar is the single minimum of 999 draws and moves 0.054 bits between seeds, which is wider than most effects being tested.

**A statistic over a field the reading selected flatters it.** Scoring only the topics a reading reached hides how few it reached. Score over the whole scheme, and report reach separately.

**Read the javadoc against the code.** `PhraseTopics.under` said *"it is a vote and not a gate"* and multiplied by `prior.shareOf(topic)`, which is `0.0` for anything the first pass missed; it removed 511 readings over 201 topics on this tree before it was fixed. `WordSpecificity` says the commonest word still votes and computes `log(1) = 0`. Where a figure is missing rather than small, suspect a contract being broken rather than a value being low. `workings.rules` now names every rule that can remove a topic with what it removed, so the next one is found by reading a file rather than by reading source.

**Check a defect is live before fixing it.** Three were killed this way in one session, each in minutes and none needing a build. A vocabulary repair that would have reached 0.22% of what carries nothing; a stated bound that no word in nine repositories ever breaks; a class of artefact that turned out to be the wrong column. The measurement that refutes a defect is usually smaller than the one that would have judged its fix.

**An absent value and a zero are different findings.** A resource that states nothing about a word, a word no resource covers, and a word every resource scores at zero are three different outcomes, and the reading is meant to keep them apart. When one is surprising, establish which of the three it is before explaining it.

## When the reading itself must move

A change in the scoring path moves every figure. Run `./gradlew read` and the `pinned` tag before and after, and keep both — `pinned` is excluded from `checkAll`, so a moved pin passes the gate unnoticed.
